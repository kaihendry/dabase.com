These were my first pair of Maui Jims and they are still my favourite. I genuinely cannot tell you
when I bought them. Long enough ago that the answer is "a different decade", and long enough that
the model number has literally worn off the frame.

Here they are on my face in the Iranian desert in August 2011, so they are at least fifteen years
old:

The reason I've kept them this long is the **glass**. Maui Jim's SuperThin Glass is heavier than
plastic and it will shatter if you're unlucky, but it is optically better than anything else you
can put in front of your eyes and it does not scratch the way polycarbonate does. Years of being
shoved into pockets and dropped on beaches, and the lenses still look through clean.

<a href="maui-jim-lens-etch.avif"><img src="maui-jim-lens-etch.avif" alt="Close-up of the Maui Jim script etched into the lens, with the bare metal temple beyond it" style="max-width:50%"></a>

That etched *Maui Jim* script is now the only identifying mark left on the whole pair. The model
number and colour code are printed inside a temple, and mine has been polished away by however many
thousand times I've folded them up.

## The bit that didn't last

The rubber nose pads perished. One was simply gone; the survivor hard and crazed. Not the frame,
not the hinges, not the lenses — the two cheapest components on the whole product.

<a href="nose-pads-detached.avif"><img src="nose-pads-detached.avif" alt="Close-up of the saddle bridge and pad arms with the loop in the middle, ruler alongside" style="max-width:50%"></a>

[Maui Jim will post you nose
pads](https://support.mauijim.com/hc/en-us/articles/12562117231511-Common-Nose-Pad-Replacement-Instructions)
free of charge, but the support flow runs on model numbers and named styles — Ho'okipa, Makaha,
Banyans — and mine isn't a style anyone can name. The first pair they sent were the chunky
rectangular sort, around 20 mm with a deep slot. My frame doesn't take that.

<a href="replacement-pads.avif"><img src="replacement-pads.avif" alt="The two replacement nose pads Maui Jim sent, chunky black rectangular blocks, next to a ruler showing roughly 20mm" style="max-width:50%"></a>

## Julie and Scott worked it out

So I sent photos, and **Julie and Scott in Maui Jim customer services** identified the part from the
shape of a pad arm on a frame nobody could name: **NSP252, "MJM Nose pads 14.3\*7 black"**, made in
Italy. They put them in the post, free, no receipt, no proof of purchase, no idea how old the
sunglasses were. They snapped straight on.

<a href="nose-pads-fixed.avif"><img src="nose-pads-fixed.avif" alt="The repaired sunglasses folded on the delivery note for part NSP252, MJM nose pads 14.3x7 black, with the new black pads fitted and a steel ruler below" style="max-width:50%"></a>

Julie, Scott — thank you. That's two envelopes and real effort spent on a customer who bought
something fifteen years ago and couldn't even tell you what it was.

**If you want sunglasses that are buy-it-for-life, buy Maui Jim.** Glass lenses that outlast
everything else on your face, and a company that will still fix them for free long after any
warranty has run out. Nobody else comes close.
