These were my first pair of Maui Jims and they are still my favourite. I genuinely cannot tell you
when I bought them. Long enough ago that the answer is "a different decade", and long enough that
the model number has literally worn off the frame.

<a href="frame-overview.avif"><img src="frame-overview.avif" alt="Maui Jim wrap sunglasses with dark metal frame photographed from above next to a steel ruler" style="max-width:50%"></a>

The reason I've kept them this long is the **glass**. Maui Jim's SuperThin Glass is the thing that
made the brand for me: it is heavier than plastic and it will shatter if you're unlucky, but it is
optically better than anything else you can put in front of your eyes and it does not scratch the
way polycarbonate does. Years of being shoved into pockets and dropped on beaches, and the lenses
still look through clean.

<a href="maui-jim-lens-etch.avif"><img src="maui-jim-lens-etch.avif" alt="Close-up of the Maui Jim script etched into the lens, with the bare metal temple beyond it" style="max-width:50%"></a>

That etched *Maui Jim* script on the lens is now the only identifying mark left on the whole pair.
Maui Jim prints the model number and colour code on the inside of a temple, and mine has been
polished away by however many thousand times I've folded them up. There's nothing to read.

## The bit that didn't last

The rubber nose pads have perished. One is simply gone; the survivor is hard, crazed, and about to
follow it. Where the missing one used to sit, the bare wire has gone green.

<a href="nose-pads-detached.avif"><img src="nose-pads-detached.avif" alt="Close-up of the saddle bridge and pad arms with the loop in the middle, ruler alongside" style="max-width:50%"></a>

This is the ordinary way good sunglasses die. Not the frame, not the hinges, not the lenses — the
two cheapest components on the whole product, the ones made of a material with a shelf life.
Everything engineered to last thirty years, mounted on something that was never going to last ten.

## Maui Jim sent the wrong pads

To their credit, [Maui Jim will post you nose
pads](https://support.mauijim.com/hc/en-us/articles/12562117231511-Common-Nose-Pad-Replacement-Instructions)
free of charge. The problem is that without a model number, nobody can work out which ones I need. Their support flow is built around three common types (NSP45, NSP3,
NSP15) mapped to named styles: Ho'okipa, Makaha, Banyans, Kanaha and so on. Mine isn't a style
anyone can name.

They sent a pair anyway. They don't fit.

<a href="replacement-pads.avif"><img src="replacement-pads.avif" alt="The two replacement nose pads Maui Jim sent, chunky black rectangular blocks, next to a ruler showing roughly 20mm" style="max-width:50%"></a>

They're the chunky rectangular NSP45-looking sort — squared-off blocks around 20 mm long with a
deep slot moulded into the back. My frame doesn't take that. Its pads are slimmer, more rounded,
and sit on a thin pad arm the slot won't close around.

<a href="wrap-profile.avif"><img src="wrap-profile.avif" alt="Side profile of the frame showing the surviving original nose pad in place, with the replacement pads out of focus behind" style="max-width:50%"></a>

You can see the difference in profile: the survivor still on the frame on the right, the pads they
sent sitting uselessly on the left.

## Where that leaves me

So I have a pair of sunglasses whose expensive parts are all fine, whose cheap parts have failed,
and which cannot be repaired because the label wore out before the product did. There is no
serial-number lookup, no lens etching that encodes the model, no QR code — nothing on the frame
that survives normal use and tells you what it is.

Next stop is a local optician with a tray of generic pads, because a good one will match by shape
rather than by part number, which is what should have happened in the first place.

**If you recognise this frame, I would love to know what it is.** Dark gunmetal full-rim wrap, wire
saddle bridge with a small oval loop in the centre, thin tapered metal temples, glass lenses. Get
in touch and I'll update this post.

A note for anyone buying now: if you're paying Maui Jim money for glass, take a photo of the inside
of the temple on day one. That printing is the only thing standing between your sunglasses and this
post.
